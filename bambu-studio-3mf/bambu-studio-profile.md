# Bambu Studio Notes - VBar Stand

The repository contains separate prepared projects:

- [Vbar_all_mounting_hardware.3mf](mounting-hardware/Vbar_all_mounting_hardware.3mf)
- [VBar-Stand.3mf](stand/VBar-Stand.3mf)

The mounting hardware can be reprinted independently of the three-color stand. The documented physical build uses PETG. Choose a suitable PETG profile for your printer and filament, then verify the selected machine, plate, nozzle and filament mapping before slicing.

## Stand color parts

The stand comprises three aligned objects: black structural core/header, colored V and edge wrap, and BAR lettering. The photographed build uses yellow for the wrap and silver for the letters. Preserve their shared origin when importing the individual STLs; do not center each part separately.

The black core already includes the keyed header. The stand project is the simplest way to retain the intended color separation.

## Before printing

- Inspect the sliced preview for missing regions, unexpected supports, color changes and gaps.
- Confirm that cap holes, pivot sleeves, keyed blocks and the M4 plunger insert bore print cleanly.
- Confirm that the stand's black core, V wrap and letters remain aligned.
- Check the 3MF's material mapping and printer settings against your own setup; do not assume those settings transfer automatically.

## Fit-sensitive details

- Left/right case bodies and caps are distinct. Their shallow `L` and `R` marks face the inside of the handle when viewed from the front of the radio.
- Use the VBar's **M3 × 10 mm cap screws**.
- A thin double-sided tape strip on top of the handle helps remove play before the caps are fitted.
- The clamped halves can feel a little loose until the stand/header joins and squares both sides. A small cap/body gap is expected.
- During final tightening, press the stand downward to ensure its header clears the bottom case corners, then cycle the full range of motion.

Follow the [assembly instructions](../documentation/assembly-instructions/assembly-instructions.md) and verify the fit of your printed parts on your own radio.
